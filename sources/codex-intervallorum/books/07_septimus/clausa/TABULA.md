# Nota Clausa

## Tabula

Seven fields follow, grown by the rule of the notae. Each carries a reading that the growth has scattered: every mark is an agent of the mould, red for one and black for nought, six to a sign. The rule is printed whole in the Regula. The key is not printed.

The key is three names, written in capitals with one space between them, in this order. The books hold all three.

The first is the intake spirit of the third book, who loses the matter at the first door and keeps the receipt.

The second is the one who, in the lines of Dante that the third book cites most nearly, sends the travellers on to a bridge that is not standing.

The third is the arcanum the Tarot of Intervals adds beyond its seventy-eight, whose office is dispersal. The Tarot was once the sixth book and is now a volume of its own.

Here the names work, and for no reason a spirit would recognise. Each is a cause in the computation and a sign to no one. Speak them wrongly and the mould grows another way.

What the seven fields carry is a disputation in seven articles. It cites only works already in the books' registers. It gives no instructions. Its digests are printed below so that a reading can be checked without trusting anyone who claims it.
