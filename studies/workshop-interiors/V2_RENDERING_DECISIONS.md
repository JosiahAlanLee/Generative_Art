# Decisions made while applying the second-pass briefs

The supplied briefs govern the new vector plates. The original diffusion-era
briefs and all first-pass results remain in the record. The written variants
are selected: two reserved clerks, a rear window and chair facing the cabinet,
one red strand stopping short, and two red terminals on black Office cords.

The maximum near silhouette in the suggested table is 0.25 × 1.30 = 0.325 mm,
which exceeds its 0.32 mm red route. The caller caps black contours at 0.30 mm
to preserve the explicitly required hierarchy. Reserved outlines remain 0.16 mm.

`allocate_hatch()` subtracts the supplied existing-ink raster itself. Passing
only residual darkness to a second pass would subtract existing ink twice.
The top-up therefore receives the full target and the cumulative existing ink.
Two rotated top-ups, rather than one, were needed to place the measured deepest
pockets in the requested 0.70–0.80 range at 600 dpi. Family pen widths match the
written 0.10 mm paths. All changes are caller-side; vendor hashes are unchanged.

Structural marks, crossing-line overlap and tiny visible face footprints make
nominal allocator density differ from measured coverage. Named caller-side
multipliers compensate for those effects. The manifest records them; the
report retains actual measurements rather than substituting target values.
The hierarchy is asserted. Target midtone values are reported alongside the
measured result, rather than claimed as exact equality.

Tone-plan measurements count hatch ink, as the common brief defines it, on
visible zone pixels in 4 mm cells. Total ink is also reported. Reserved-region
checks exclude only content expressly permitted there (rules, cage/filaments,
or a clerk hand), and report raw ink as well. The halo limits concern competing
hatch; ink belonging to the strand or the plain recess rule is not an omitted
hatch failure.

The Office brief contains conflicting geometry statements: it says the recess
is larger than the cord gap, while its explicit checks require the gap to clear
the recess as before. The construction follows the explicit checks: free ends
at z = 2.50 and 6.14, recess from z = 2.85 to 5.82. The 3.64-unit gap therefore
clears the complete reserved rectangle. No mark bridges it.

The Cabinet's folded board rests shut on an inclined support so that its grid
can be seen from the required low camera. It is not laid out for play. The
foreground bench is moved into the field so the spool, detached belt and crank
can be inspected at the stated print size.

The Hand's page surface is flattened because the first raised page arch hid
parts of its rules at the close camera angle. Its sheaf is matched in an
invertible projected coordinate system; a literal geometric intersection
check accompanies the no-occlusion check. There are 23 row identities across
the opening: 22 receive a strand and one remains unreached.

No plot-master variant or physical plot test is claimed. The delivered PDF is
a print-placement study; it does not replace or bind the book's selected art.

The Cabinet through-view measurement intersects the projected front and rear
end caps of the declared clear volume. This identifies rays that traverse its
full depth, rather than rays merely touching its projected silhouette and then
meeting a dark side panel or roof. The raw coverage and window area in that
inspection corridor are reported. The crank has a separate visible-pixel span
check, so a large source crank hidden behind a door cannot pass as legible.
