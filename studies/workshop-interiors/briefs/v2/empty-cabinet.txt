THE EMPTY CABINET / brief, second pass
Interlude leaf, De Vinculis, before Operation VI. Printed 152 x 203 mm, 3:4,
inside an unclosed printer's rule. Text it serves (Commentarium VI): "The
cabinet illustration is empty. No pictured body is assigned the role of hidden
servant." The plate's argument is the emptiness. Everything else is there to
make the emptiness read.

THE SCENE
A box that has been opened, not a frame. An eighteenth-century mechanical
cabinet, taller than wide, with SOLID side panels, a solid top and a solid
base; two front doors swung fully open on their hinges, their outer faces
carrying the ornament; two side inspection hatches hinged upward and propped,
leaving open apertures in the panels. Inside: nothing. The perimeter mechanics
(pulleys in bearing blocks, a drive belt, the axle to a crank on the right side
at hand height) run along the inside edges of the frame and never cross the
central volume. A chessboard folded shut lies on top.

Behind the cabinet, centred on the rear wall, a window: mullions in black,
panes reserved paper. Seen through the open front, the window fills the empty
volume with light. That is the device: the void is the brightest region on the
plate because the room beyond it is.

Foreground, lower right: a workbench with an open ledger of blank ruled
columns, a pencil, a spool of vermilion thread with a loose end, and a detached
drive belt lying on the bench beside the ledger (not on the floor). Left: a
plain chair, unoccupied, four legs on the floor. Bookcases on the SIDE walls
only; nothing on the rear wall but the window and plaster.

CAMERA
Three-quarter from front-left, eye at the cabinet's mid-height (about the sill
of the lower side hatch), so the open front faces the viewer and the window is
seen through it. Field about 38 degrees. The cabinet occupies at least a third
of the plate's width.

READING ORDER
1 the lit void inside the cabinet
2 the open doors and raised hatches (the fact of opening)
3 the mechanics at the edges, and the crank
4 the ledger and the red thread
5 the chair
The bookcases are the lowest-ranked tone on the plate.

TONE PLAN
  interior faces of the side panels, seen through the hatches   0.65-0.75
  underside of the cabinet top, seen from below                 0.60
  outer faces of the doors (ornament over a mid tone)           0.40-0.50
  cabinet exterior panels                                        0.45

  bookcases (side walls)                                         0.25-0.30

     the window panes, seen through the cabinet and above it
     the pages of the open ledger (rules only)
     the top face of the folded chessboard (grid only)
The void is defined by contrast: the darkest faces on the plate are the inside
surfaces of the box, and what they surround is the lightest.

INK
Black for everything but the thread. Vermilion: the thread on the spool and its
loose end, 0.32 mm. No brass; the door ornament is black at ornament weight.

WHAT CHANGES FROM THE FIRST PASS
- Solid panels and solid doors replace the open frame; a frame can be looked
  through but not inspected, and the plate is about inspection.
- The window replaces the rear-wall shelf banks that were filling the void.
- Crank and pulleys enlarged until they read at 152 mm; the crank is the one
  mechanism the eye must find.
- The drive belt comes off the floor onto the bench; on the floor it reads as
  a stain.
- The cabinet's turned feet and crown finials are cut to what is visible at
  printed size (the first pass has 56 foot cylinders; four will do).

CHECKS
- Void: the 70 inspection rays, as before.
- The region seen through the open front (project the clear volume to the
  page) has mean coverage < 0.08 and is lower than every cabinet panel.
- Chair: four floor contacts, as before.
- Window panes: coverage < 0.02; at least one pane visible through the front
  opening.
- Hierarchy as measured: interior panel faces > door faces > bench > bookcases
  > rear plaster.
- Crank: its silhouette at printed size spans >= 6 mm.

AUTHOR'S DECISIONS
A. A window behind the cabinet (as written) or plain reserved plaster. The
   window is recommended; plain plaster also reads but with less force.
B. Whether the chair faces the cabinet or the reader. It faces the cabinet
   here, so that it is a place someone has left, not a seat offered.
