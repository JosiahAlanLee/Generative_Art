THE MAINTAINED WORKSHOP / brief, second pass
Frontispiece, Clavicula Matricis et Intervallorum. Printed 156 x 208 mm, 3:4,
inside an unclosed printer's rule. Caption it must answer:
"One hand checks the instrument. Another keeps the room warm."

THE SCENE
A nocturnal library-workshop cut away on two levels. A raised work gallery on
timber supports, with an open service bay beneath it and access stairs at the
right. On the gallery, left: a brass observation wheel on two pillars, its
centre open, a removable ruler resting on a bracket below the hub. Right: a
glass reserve vessel hung from the ceiling on a yoke, directly above a small
iron stove whose door stands open. A flue leaves the stove and rises to the
ceiling. Between wheel and vessel: an open instrument gate (two jambs, one leaf
swung out) and, beyond it, an unfinished shelf board carried on one trestle,
its far end in the air. Bookcases on the rear wall either side; sloped shelves
of blank folded records on the lower level. Branching filaments in the plaster
and inside the glass.

The red route: one continuous vermilion line from the wheel's hub, through the
open gate, to the end of the unfinished shelf, where it stops. Nothing else is
red.

HANDS (author's decision; see end)
Two clerks, built as paper-reserved silhouettes: hooded, no face, no features,
outline only, each with one drawn hand. The left clerk's hand rests on the
ruler. The right clerk's hand holds the stove door. Their bodies carry no
hatching and no ornament; they are the two largest reserved shapes on the
plate after the stove mouth and the vessel. They are present because the
caption names them; they are reserved because the series does not draw faces.

CAMERA
Three-quarter from front-left, not frontal. Eye height a little above the
gallery floor (about 1.6 m over the deck), so the gallery reads as a stage and
the service bay reads as the space under it. Horizontal field about 40
degrees. The wheel is the largest single object; the vessel is second.

READING ORDER
1 the wheel (darkest instrument, left)
2 the red route leaving it
3 the gate and the shelf end where the route stops
4 the vessel (bright) above the stove mouth (brightest)
5 the two hands
Nothing in the room may outrank these: bookcases, filaments and the stairs are
supporting tone.

The stove mouth and the open wheel centre are the two brightest points, one on
each side; the plate is lit from the stove, and the hatch on the stove side of
every object is lighter than on the far side.

INK
Black: all structure, bookcases, tracery, filaments, ruler and wheel ticks.
Vermilion: the route only, 0.32 mm, drawn last and unbroken by thin tracery.
No brass.

WHAT CHANGES FROM THE FIRST PASS
- Not frontal; the frontal camera flattened it.
- Clerks in, as reserved silhouettes, or the caption changes.
- The stove must read as a heat source: open door, reserved mouth, flue.
- The vessel hangs where the stove's heat reaches it, visibly.
- Floor lines stop at the rule; nothing extends past the room.
- The wheel does not overlap the bookcase at the hub; move the shelf bank so
  the wheel sits against plain plaster.

CHECKS (in addition to the first pass's)
- Red route: one connected polyline; printed length >= 60 mm; at no point
  occluded for more than 3 mm; the heaviest line on the plate.
- Reserved regions listed above: coverage < 0.02 each.
- Hierarchy: mean coverage of the service bay > stove > bookcases > rear wall
  centre > gallery deck, as measured, not as intended.
- Two silhouettes present, each with exactly one hand stroke group, no face
  strokes inside the hood outline.
- Legibility at printed size: 600 dpi binary proof shows the ruler ticks and
  the wheel ticks as separate marks.

AUTHOR'S DECISIONS
A. Hands as reserved silhouettes (as written here), or no hands and a caption
   that does not name them. The plate and the caption must agree.
B. Whether the night is in the plate (corner vignette) or the frontispiece is
   timeless; the tone plan assumes night.
