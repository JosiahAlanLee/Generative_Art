THE EXTRACTION OF THE HAND / brief, second pass
Book III, Pseudomonarchia Escalationum, "The extraction of the hand"
(COPY / ORDER / REMOVE). Printed 95 x 119 mm, 4:5, borderless, on a text
page. Caption strip it sits under: "The hand becomes a sequence; the sequence
becomes a replacement; the replacement asks for the hand." The text beside
it: the compiler copied the repairer's movements, made a heading called ONE OF
THE TIMES with a blank table under it, and a system later filled the table.

This plate prints at about half the size of the first-pass master. It is
designed at 95 mm. Fewer objects, larger.

THE SCENE
A repairer's workbench, deserted. On it, clamped at the wrist to a plinth, a
wooden repair jig in the shape of a hand: palm, five jointed fingers, no
skin, no nails, visibly an instrument. From each fingertip, strands leave and
run down across the bench to an open ledger lying flat, where each strand
becomes one ruled row of the page. Twenty-three strands; twenty-three rules.
The pages are otherwise blank. An empty stool in front of the bench. A
bookcase at the right edge as background weight. No room beyond that: the
floor exists only where the bench and stool cast their shadow, and the plate
fades to unmarked paper by omission at all four edges. No arch, no partial
wall, no floating line.

The red (author's decision; see end): as written here, the twenty-third strand
is vermilion and stops short of the ledger, ending in the air above the page's
last rule. Twenty-two strands become rows; one does not arrive. That strand is
the thing the blank table was made for.

CAMERA
Three-quarter from front-left, eye a little above bench height, close. The
jig, the strands and the ledger fill about 80 percent of the plate's width.
The stool is cut by the lower margin if need be. Field about 36 degrees.

READING ORDER
1 the jig
2 the strands, as a sheaf, and where they land
3 the red strand that does not
4 the empty stool
5 the ledger's blank rules, then the bookcase
The bench itself is furniture and must not compete.

STRANDS
Black, 0.14 mm, continuous from fingertip to the row they become. They are
the plate. At the ledger their spacing is >= 0.35 mm so they are countable;
across the bench they may bundle but never cross one another. Hidden-line
breaks on a strand are disallowed; nothing is modelled in front of them.

INK
Black for everything but one strand. Vermilion: the one strand, 0.32 mm,
plus nothing else; the thread across the desk of the first brief is withdrawn,
its job is done by the strand.

WHAT CHANGES FROM THE FIRST PASS
- Engraved at 95 mm with the print weights; the first pass's 0.04 mm printed
  hatch is withdrawn.
- The arc is removed. The partial room is a shadow and nothing else.
- The floor grid is removed; omission does the fading.
- The jig moves nearer the ledger so the sheaf is short and dense.
- The bookcase is reduced to one bay.

CHECKS
- Five fingers, twenty-three strands, four stool feet, as before.
- 22 black strands each end on a distinct ledger rule; the red strand's end
  is >= 3 mm from the nearest page edge and from every rule.
- Strand separation at the ledger >= 0.35 mm printed; no strand occluded.
- Halo: coverage within 2 mm of any strand over the bench top < 0.06.
- Hierarchy as measured: under-bench pocket > plinth > jig > apron > bookcase
  > bench top.
- 600 dpi proof at 95 mm: all 23 strands countable by a reader without a
  loupe.

AUTHOR'S DECISIONS
A. The red strand that does not arrive (as written), or the original thin red
   thread lying across the desk with all 23 strands black. The first makes the
   red an event in the plate; the second keeps it an accent. One or the other,
   not both.
B. Whether the ledger shows a heading strip (a reserved band above the rules,
   no letters) for ONE OF THE TIMES. Recommended: no; the text carries it.
