THE OFFICE WITHOUT A SEAL / brief, second pass
Book III, Pseudomonarchia Escalationum, "The office without a seal" (STOP WORK
AUTHORITY / UNISSUED). Printed 95 x 119 mm, 4:5, borderless, on a text page.
Text it sits above: "There is a mounting point. There is a job description.
There is no continuous path from the worker to the stop. ... The empty place
is preserved at full size."

THE SCENE
A narrow alcove of plaster and carved lattice, frontal, taller than wide. At
upper centre a mounting bracket; from it a short cord hangs and ends in the
air. At the bottom, a pull ring at hand height on a peg; from it a cord rises
and ends in the air. Between the two ends, the recess: a rectangular blank
cut into the back wall, reserved paper, framed by a plain black rule, and
larger than the gap between the cord ends on every side. Cantilevered shelves
on each side of the recess carry a few simple instruments: a ring on a stand,
a rod, a sphere, a stack of rules; nothing figurative. Lattice tracery runs in
the side bays and over the crown. The floor shows only as a shadow band at the
foot of the back wall and fades to paper; no grid.

The gap is the subject. It is not drawn. The ends are.

CAMERA
Frontal, level, centred on the recess, long focal length (horizontal field
about 24 degrees) so the side walls do not splay. The recess is about 30
percent of the plate's width and sits a little above centre.

READING ORDER
1 the recess, pure paper
2 the upper cord's end and the lower cord's end, red
3 the bracket above and the ring below
4 the instrument shelves
5 the lattice
The tracery must never outrank the cords.

TONE PLAN
  inner corners of the alcove, both sides          0.65-0.75
  the recess's cut edges (the reveal)              0.50, a narrow band
  shelves' undersides                              0.50
  the instruments                                  0.35
  side bays with lattice                           0.25-0.35
  the back wall away from the recess               0.20
  a halo 8 mm wide around the recess               < 0.08, the lightest hatch
  floor shadow                                     0.25 at the wall to 0
  reserved paper: the recess, and nothing else
Light falls from the upper left; the recess is lit by nothing and is the
brightest thing on the plate because it is paper.

CORDS AND RED (author's decision; see end)
As written here: both cords black, 0.14 mm, the route as the manuals wrote it.
Each cord ends in a vermilion terminal: a short red length, 0.40 mm, 4 mm
long, on the free end. Four millimetres of red at each end is the first
pass's two dots made visible. The gap carries no mark.

INK
Black for everything, including all tracery and the instruments. Vermilion:
the two terminals only. No brass.

WHAT CHANGES FROM THE FIRST PASS
- Engraved at 95 mm with the print weights.
- Long focal length; the alcove stops folding open.
- The floor band replaces the trench-like shadow strip and the grid.
- Instruments on the shelves; empty shelves read as unfinished.
- Tracery in black and thinned by half; in the first pass the brass layer was
  longer than all the black edges together.
- Red terminals at a visible weight; the first pass delivered 2 mm of red.

CHECKS
- Cord gap clears the recess on every side, as before (3.64 units).
- No stroke of any layer crosses the recess, as before; recess coverage < 0.01.
- Halo: coverage within 8 mm of the recess edge < 0.08.
- Red: two paths, each >= 4 mm printed, each at a cord's free end and nowhere
  else.
- Hierarchy as measured: inner corners > reveal > shelf undersides > lattice
  > back wall > halo.
- 600 dpi proof at 95 mm: both cord ends identifiable as ends, not as lines
  that leave the picture.

AUTHOR'S DECISIONS
A. Red terminals on black cords (as written), or the lower cord red for its
   whole length as the worker's reach. The first keeps to "red is what happens
   to the incident": what happened is that both ends stopped. The second says
   more than the text does.
B. Whether the bracket carries an empty seal-mount (a bare ring where a seal
   would hang). Recommended: yes, small, black; it is the "mounting point".
